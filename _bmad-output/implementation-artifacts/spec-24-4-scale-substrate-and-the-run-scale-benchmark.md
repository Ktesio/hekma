## Intent

**Problem:** Hekma claims every surface holds at a million Runs, and NFR-4's amendment says the placeholders are replaced "after the Epic 24 benchmark" — but the substrate cannot hold that load today. Every snapshot publish clones the whole `RunSnapshot` (`RunSnapshot::with` calls `self.clone()`), so at 1M open Runs each writer batch pays ~1M map copies on its ack path. Ended Runs never leave `state.db`: their rows, labels and latches accumulate forever, and 21-5's listing window is a read-side filter that hides rows without removing them — explicitly a lie until compaction exists. The calls store is one SQLite file that grows forever (today's `segment_path` even keeps writing the oldest segment). And AD-18's 2026-10-30 amendment fired reopen trigger (4): the archived per-instance lock plan's Steps 0–4 are activated, the coarse locks still serialize supervision, listing pages still assemble under the registry mutex, and the healthy-count walk still visits every open Run per listing. Nothing measures any of this at 10k / 100k / 1M Runs.

**Approach:** Four substrates, one benchmark. (1) **AD-18 Steps 0–4** executed as archived, re-anchored to today's code: `EnginePaths` hoisted out of `Registry` behind an `Arc`; the whole-file agent-log reads moved off the registry guard; a `TimedMutex` layer with an adversarial 25-instance fixture and the `interaction.rs` isolation bound tightened from the encoded-acceptable 9 s to AI-59's documented bound; named `critical()` scopes with a symbol-anchored debt ratchet in CI; the `StoreGate` split of mixed DB+FS registry methods (the recursive Agent Home deletion moves off the gate); and `Supervisor` split into `SupervisorShared` + per-instance `InstanceState` behind the one lock — Steps 0–4 only, the cell (Step 5) is not taken. (2) **The snapshot publish becomes O(changed entries)**: the publisher keeps an immutable base plus a bounded delta log; a publish appends only its changed entries; a re-base folds deltas into a new base on an amortized bound; readers still take one lock-free `Arc` clone, and the snapshot still holds only non-ended Runs. (3) **Ended-Run compaction and calls-store rotation**: ended Runs older than the retention window (default 7 days, now configurable) are compacted into a `compacted_runs` rollup that keeps run id, usage totals, labels and end cause, then their detail rows are deleted — never a Run whose subtree still has open descendants; calls segments rotate on the UTC date boundary and are dropped whole past their own retention window, crash-safely, with the hash-only byte-scan proof extended to rotated and dropped segments. (4) **The Run-scale benchmark**: the perf harness gains a `--run-scale <N>` mode that seeds N Runs with no processes (21-5's seed mix plus owner rows), gates the first `hekma ps` page < 1 s strict and a publish-cost gate that must NOT scale with N, rides 23-1's proxy scenario to re-measure ≤ 10 ms p99 added latency at each rung's open-Run count, and records the 10k / 100k / 1M ladder against NFR-4's two placeholder numbers in `docs/testing.md`; 10k and 100k run in the CI perf job, 1M is recorded per the owner's ruling. The listing's own scale fixes land because the 1M gate demands them: a covering index for the healthy count, selector arms that use the partial indexes, and listing reads off the registry mutex.

## Design Notes

- **What this story owns, and what it rides.** AD-18's 2026-10-30 amendment activated the archived plan's Steps 0–4 (the story's first bullet); this spec executes them as archived with today's re-anchoring, honoring every abort condition, and does NOT take Step 5 (the cell) — reopen trigger (4) activates Steps 0–4, and the per-instance model itself stays deferred behind the standing triggers. The snapshot, compaction and calls-store halves implement AD-25/AD-27's ruled text ("structurally shared or sharded so a publish costs O(changed entries)"; "kept for a configurable window, then compacted into a compacted-Run rollup … their `runs`, `run_labels` and detail rows are deleted"; "SQLite files rotated by time, dropped whole at retention"). The benchmark realizes NFR-4's amendment and SM-8. Everything rides the merged chain: 22-1 (built), 23-1 (in build — its proxy scenario, calls store and `--proxy` bench task are the seams; if its scenario names or gate shapes drift when it merges, ride them and note it in the Spec Change Log), and builds after 24-3 in the Epic 24 order.
- **Steps 0–4, re-anchored (the plan's line numbers are all stale).** The archived plan was authored 2026-08-27 against `supervisor.rs` as a monolith and a world with no store writer and no Run registry; since then 21-1 moved the AD-7 pipeline into the writer (off these locks), and 21-2 split Run state out of `Supervisor` (so Step 4's `InstanceState` is smaller than planned). What remains true: `engine.rs` still has 113 `.lock()` sites (not 37), `EnginePaths` still lives inside `Registry` (`domain/registry.rs:259`), `agent_home` still takes the registry mutex on the caller's thread under a comment claiming purity (`engine.rs:795`), `read_agent_log` still does whole-file `read_to_string` under the guard it is handed (`interaction.rs:288`), the unix `Drop` is already fixed (`try_wait`, per the amendment — the plan's Windows correction stands, CloseHandle-only), and `interaction.rs:819/:837` still encode AI-59's 4.31 s coupling as acceptable at 9 s. The spec carries the open halves only. The steps keep the plan's own abort conditions verbatim (Step 2: symbol-anchored debt or fix the anchoring; Step 3: the histogram must show max gate hold falling; Step 4: a cross-instance invariant stops the sequence) — a stop is a recorded outcome, not a failure.
- **Why Steps 0–4 belong to a scale story.** They are the substrate the ladder measures: at 1M Runs the coarse locks guard a much larger surface (listings, snapshot loads, compaction batches), and AD-17's bounded-work rule is load-bearing there. The steps also carry the specific bounded-work fixes the amendment names. They are independently shippable and revertible (the plan's own risk asymmetry), so they land as the story's first commits, one step per commit, each gated.
- **The delta-log publish (chosen over the alternatives).** The requirement is AD-25's: O(changed) publish, one publisher, lock-free readers, only non-ended Runs. A sharded map (clone only touched shards) is O(shard size) per publish — not O(changed). A persistent tree (im-style HAMT) is O(log n) per change but adds a dependency (NFR-8) or a hand-rolled trie's risk. The delta log is std-only and honestly O(changed) per publish: base + bounded layers, tombstones for ended entries and removed keys, re-base folded when the log exceeds `max(4096, base/32)` — amortized O(changed) with each entry re-based at most once per ~4096 changes, and a synchronous re-base bounded at ~1M pointer copies (tens of ms) inside the writer's post-commit path, inside the 250 ms batch and 1 s ack bounds (21-1's gates re-measured at scale by the ladder). Readers resolve newest-first through ≤ 32 layers worst case (a handful of map probes on a path that already does map lookups); the `RevokedSet`'s time eviction rides the re-base. The trade is stated: worst-case one publish carries a re-base; the ladder's publish gate measures exactly that p99.
- **Compaction eligibility and the ended-Run seam.** A Run is compactable iff ended, outside the window, and free of open descendants — the PRD keeps an ended Run's ceilings binding its open children ("until its last child ends"), and AD-27's rollup keeps no limits or parent link, so compacting a Run with open descendants would silently drop enforceable ceilings and break the chain walk. The check rides `idx_runs_parent`. Totals move from the Run's own `usage_rollup_run` row (which 21-2 already maintains per Run, ancestors included), so no re-summing ever happens (AD-25's rule). What is deleted: the Run's registry detail rows; `tokens` rows cascade with the Run (22-1's audit trail lives in the outbox events, which stay). What is NOT deleted without O2: `usage_events` (billing evidence — the archived plan §5.5 explicitly reserves retention-vs-evidence as a product call; the recommendation deletes them at compaction because the rollup keeps totals and the calls store keeps the per-call view, but the owner may keep them). Events/outbox rows are never deleted here; outbox pruning is its own scale question (new deferral, below).
- **One window, one owner (the 21-5 seam).** 21-5 shipped the window as a read-side constant with the knob and compaction deferred here, reasoning that "a knob with no enforcement behind it would hide rows without removing them". This story lands the enforcement, so the knob ships with it: one stored setting drives the listing cutoff AND the compaction pass (they can never disagree), default 7 days (the PRD's assumed default, validated by 21-5). The calls window is a second field on the same row: per-call records are evidence for 19.4's read surface and a different cost curve than Run detail, so the owner may want them to diverge (O1). Both are public config surface (config keys + CLI + API) — flagged for confirmation.
- **Rotation, crash-safety and the head-segment drift.** 23-1's `segment_path` picks the lexicographically FIRST segment — with rotation this would keep writing the oldest file; the head rule becomes today's-or-newest. Rotation happens between writer batches, so a call's `open_calls` row and its closing record land in one segment; the open-time closure scans every retained segment (bounded: at most one per day per window), so an `open_calls` row stranded in an aged segment by a crash mid-rotation is still closed. Drop-whole is AD-27's rule: no per-row deletes, checkpoint then unlink `.db` + `-wal` + `-shm`, never a segment holding a live owner's `open_calls` row, idempotent under a crash (the next pass re-scans). Date-only rotation (no size cap) keeps the 23-1 naming honest (`<date>.db`); a same-day size rotation would need suffixed names — deferred (below).
- **The benchmark's shape (and what "seeded without agents" means).** Seeding writes rows straight through the store exactly as `--ps-seed` does today (no processes, Windows-safe): Runs, two labels each, `run_policy` resolutions, subtree rollups, and — closing 21-5 L7 — one open owner per instance plus ended owners inside the window, at 21-5's documented status mix. Estimated cost on a dev machine: 10k ≈ seconds, 100k ≈ 1–2 min, 1M ≈ 5–10 min (3–4M rows bulk-inserted in one transaction per table), measurement ≈ 1 min per rung (10 iterations at 1M) — the whole local ladder ≈ 20 min. The CI perf job gains 100k in the consolidated scenario step (a few added minutes on the ubuntu runner, no new job); 1M is the owner's call (O3) — recommended manual-or-nightly with the numbers recorded, because a per-push 1M seed (~10 min + ~1 GiB of database) is CI-minutes and disk the ratchet does not need. Gates are absolute with the house once-retry (the canonical baseline carries no scenario metrics — the mode-aware rule — so the ladder never ratchets; the DEFAULT gates keep ratcheting against `benches/baselines/perf-ubuntu-x86_64.json` unchanged).

Replacing the PRD's placeholder text with the measured budgets is the owner's PRD edit after acceptance (AI-58: this spec edits no planning artifact); the spec's AC only records and maps.
