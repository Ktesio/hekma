PROLOGUE

The spark

The session opened on a sentence from you that the rest of the evening kept answering.

Your opening brief

  • your directionBudget block and pause alone are not enough reason to adopt Hekma. OpenCode and Hermes are individual-level agents; businesses build with LangGraph and CrewAI, and they fear a prod workflow pausing on cost. Keep the individuals. Pivot from gatekeeper to external management for running, managing, observing and supervising agentic workloads.

Goal of the session: a value proposition businesses take seriously, LangGraph and CrewAI teams and the prod-pause fear included, without losing individual users.

Four forks in the road, carried in as inputs

  1. Process boundary or in-framework?Does Hekma stand outside the agent, or inside its code?
  2. Where it stands among neighboursGateways, observability, durable execution, orchestration.
  3. The prod-pause fearTurn a single stop switch into a policy ladder.
  4. Individuals and enterprise on one coreA solo laptop and a business fleet, one product.
  • your callCoach stance: Creative Partner. Techniques: Role Playing, What If Scenarios, Nature's Solutions.
ACT I · ROLE PLAYING

Five voices walk into the control room

Each one wanted something different from Hekma. You voiced three of them yourself; the coach played the SRE and, at your request, the compliance officer. About fifty ideas came out of it.

SRE

The on-call SRE

"One run is on fire. Don't make me restart the building."

voiced by the coach
  • coachControl at the run level, not the process level: find the one misbehaving run (its step, its customer) and act on just that run.
  • coachA cost pause is worse than a crash for a prod workflow. A crash restarts; a pause strands. Hekma's default for prod should keep the work moving.
  • coachKill one run now and replay it later from where it stopped: a checkpoint-aware stop instead of a pause.
VP

The VP of Engineering

"Boring, in the best possible way."

voiced by you

Your wishlist, in the VP's voice

  • your ideaPlugs easily into our existing observability landscape.
  • your ideaEasy operator control over how Hekma behaves.
  • your ideaNo unexpected shutdowns of agentic workflows in prod.
  • your ideaNo redeploys to change a Hekma config: config changes live.
  • your ideaControls a CrewAI workflow with little or no integration work.
  • your ideaPause and restart a workflow from a checkpoint, safely.
  • your ideaBlast-radius isolation: acting on one flow never breaks other flows, work or critical paths.

The coach's answers

  • coachPause at the wire: Hekma holds the workflow's next model call instead of signalling the process. The workflow is suspended mid-step with state intact, and a base-URL override means zero code change.
  • coachRelease, reroute (to a cheaper model) or reject the held call later. A resume is just letting the call through.
  • coachPolicy as live data: budgets and rules change through the Control API or a watched policy file, versioned and audited, with no redeploy.
  • coachShadow mode: a new policy runs observe-only first and reports what it would have done before it may act in prod.
  • coach insight · tensionZero integration versus checkpoint. From outside the process Hekma cannot checkpoint framework state, but the model wire is the zero-code seam where a hold acts like a checkpoint.

The agent itself

"Tell me my limits up front, and I'll carry them myself."

voiced by you
  • your ideaCrewAI Flows and LangGraph graphs send their own flow policy with each outgoing request to Hekma's gateway (max budget, max rpm and so on) plus a flow identifier. Hekma's rules become dynamic, declared by the framework.
  • your ideaHekma-level non-negotiable standard policies that callers cannot override, or can only where Hekma's config allows it.
# the flow declares
POST /v1/chat/completions
x-hekma-flow: claims-intake
x-hekma-policy: budget=4.00; rpm=30
# Hekma answers, like rate-limit headers
200 OK
x-hekma-budget-remaining: 2.71
x-hekma-policy-id: ops/claims@v3

Header names invented for this keepsake.

  • coachTwo-layer policy: callers declare, operators bound. A caller may only tighten an operator rule, never loosen it, unless the operator delegates headroom (like IAM permission boundaries or a Kubernetes LimitRange).
  • coachBudget headers on every response (remaining tokens or dollars, reset, policy id), so the agent self-paces without any SDK.
  • coachAsk, don't work around: on exhaustion Hekma answers with a typed response that names how to request more, routed to auto-approve rules or a human.
  • coachA policy header standard: publish the request-policy header as an open format that CrewAI, LangGraph and AutoGen can emit, with Hekma as the reference enforcer.

The compliance officer, and the auditor

"Show me who could stop it, and prove what it didn't do."

voiced by the coach, at your request
  • your directionNot familiar with the EU AI Act, so you asked the coach to voice the compliance and security officer.
  • coach insightHekma is not itself a high-risk AI system, but its customers (deployers) need logging, with logs kept at least six months (AI Act Art. 12 and 26), and human oversight including the ability to stop (Art. 14). Hekma's pause, stop and drain already are an oversight control. Dates are in flux, and this is not legal advice.
  • coachTamper-evident run ledger: a hash-chained record of every model call, tool call, policy decision and policy change, with retention and export.
  • coachOversight as a feature: approval gates on high-impact tool calls (pay, email, delete) with a named human approver recorded in the ledger.
  • coachEgress and residency control at the wire: per-flow allow-lists of providers and regions, non-EU endpoints blocked, optional PII redaction before the call leaves.
  • coachEvidence pack on demand: a per-flow report for an auditor covering runs, models, spend, interventions, who approved what, and policy history.
  • coachFour-eyes policy changes: in regulated mode a live policy edit needs a second approver, and every change lands in the ledger.

The auditor's three fears, as you named them

1

⬢ your idea

The agent sends a wrong application approval to a customer.

2

⬢ your idea

The agent sends letters or emails to customers it should not.

3

⬢ your ideascariest

Cross-process data contamination: a process for customer X gets data from a process for customer Y, and leaks it.

  • coach insightAll three fears are about the wire. Irreversible customer-facing actions and cross-subject data flow both pass through a point Hekma can sit on.
  • coachCustomer-facing outbox: every outbound to a customer (email, letter, decision) goes through a Hekma outbox with policy: hold, sample N% for review, human approve, or a recall window before release.
  • coachSubject-scoped runs: each run declares its data subject (customer id); Hekma blocks and alerts when prompts, tool arguments or outputs carry another subject's identifiers.
  • coachContamination canaries: seed a unique fake marker per run. If run A's canary ever shows up in run B's traffic, contamination is proven and both runs freeze.
  • coachProving the negative: the ledger plus subject checks let Hekma state, per customer run, that no other customer's identifiers crossed the wire.
  • your ideaLeak paths: the vector store, shared memory, and many more.
  • your idea · hardest riskBad input: a CrewAI or LangGraph flow starts with incorrect data, or agents call tools with the wrong request parameters.
  • coach insightHekma cannot judge whether data is correct, but it can judge whether it is consistent with what the run declared. Consistency is checkable at the wire; correctness is not.

The coach's answer to bad input: bind the run

  • coachRun binding: at start a run declares its binding (customer_id, application_id); every tool call and outbound must reference the same binding or be held. One mechanism for wrong parameters and contamination.
  • coachTool-argument contracts at the wire: per-tool rules, such as send_email.to belongs to the bound customer, approve_application.id equals the bound application, amount within range.
  • coachIntake preflight: the flow declares an input schema, and Hekma validates the starting payload before the first model call and refuses bad starts.
  • coachQuarantine and resume: on a binding or contract violation, freeze the run and hold its outbox, let a human fix the input, and resume from the last checkpoint without touching other runs.
ACT II · WHAT IF SCENARIOS

Four detonations

Blow up one assumption at a time and see what survives. About thirty entries came out of the rubble.

  • your insightHekma is open source, so "worth paying for" doesn't apply directly. The bar is worth adopting.

What if #1Hekma enforced nothing at all?

What survived the blast

  • your ideaOne consistent interface for any agentic workload. Install it anywhere (local, a cloud function, EC2) or connect to Hekma endpoints, then manage, observe, pause, resume, stop, budget and emit telemetry the same way whether it runs CrewAI, LangGraph, Hermes or a plain LLM call. No new tooling when the agent tech changes.
  • your ideaFor individuals: Hekma nodes over a tailnet give one global view of, and control over, the whole agentic estate from a single installation.
  • coach insightEnforcement becomes one optional verb. The core value is a uniform control plane over mixed agent runtimes, what kubectl and Docker did for processes and containers.
  • coachSame verbs everywhere: hekma ps, logs, top, pause, resume, stop and budget work identically for every runtime.
  • coachThe swap-the-engine promise: move a flow from CrewAI to LangGraph and its dashboards, policies, alerts and runbooks stay unchanged. Portability is the pitch.
  • coachNodes and a hub: every box runs hekma serve; one hub, or peer federation over a tailnet, aggregates runs, spend and controls across laptop, home server, VPS and cloud.
  • coach questionThe business model for open-source Hekma stays open: adoption first, monetisation out of scope for this session.

What if #2Hekma could only attach, and never launch anything?

What survived the blast

  • your insightAttach-only, control weakens: Hekma may fail to control the other process. What's left is the wire.
  • your insightAttach-only Hekma loses an accurate hekma ps. It has to wait for incoming requests to discover running agents.
  • coach insightWire control is stronger than it looks. An agent that cannot reach models or tools is effectively paused, and with key custody it also cannot bypass. The Hermes bypass fix and attach-mode control are the same feature.
  • coachThe control-depth ladder made explicit: observed < wire < registered < supervised, with a CONTROL column per run in hekma ps.
  • coachLease-based presence: attached runs hold a lease renewed by heartbeat or traffic. An expired lease shows as silent, not gone, so ps stays honest without spawning anything.
  • coachSelf-announce: frameworks register a run on start (a header, an OTel span or one SDK call), turning wire-discovered runs into registered runs.

What if #3A run lasted months?

What survived the blast

  • your principleRun duration must not change how a workload runs, unless it breaches a Hekma policy.
  • your insight · riskIf Hekma crashes it takes the processes it owns (hekma run) with it. Registered runs keep going.
  • your ideaResilience and safe recovery when Hekma crashes with owned processes, for example by running them detached.
  • coachShim-per-run supervision, the containerd-shim and tmux-server pattern: a tiny per-run shim owns the process and its pipes, so the Hekma daemon can crash, restart or upgrade and reattach to every shim.
  • coachDurable run registry: runs, budgets and leases are journaled to disk, so a restart recovers ps, spend so far and holds exactly where they were.
  • coachZero-downtime Hekma upgrades under months-long runs, without restarting them.
  • coachWindowed budgets (per day, per month) for long runs, not only per-run totals.
  • coach questionPolicy versioning: does a run started under policy v3 follow v5 when ops change it, or stay pinned? Proposed: pinned by default, opt-in follow-latest, and the ledger records which.

What if #4It were 10,000+ runs across 200 machines?

What survived the blast

  • your insightAt 10,000+ runs the CLI is for emergencies only, and it needs pagination, limits and filtering. Day to day is the API for actions and the observability provider's dashboards, fed by Hekma's OTel.
  • your ideaScale target: 10,000 or 10,000,000 runs should work smoothly. Hekma must scale to millions of runs.
  • coach insightPositioning: OTel-first, UI-optional. Hekma is the control plane and the telemetry source, not another dashboard to adopt.
  • coachAct on sets, not runs: label selectors for every verb, like kubectl (hekma pause --selector flow=claims,region=eu).
  • coachException-first views: by default show only runs that are anomalous, held, over budget or silent. Healthy runs are counts.
  • coachHierarchical rollups: nodes aggregate locally and hubs merge summaries, so control and counts scale without central per-call traffic.
  • coachOTel cardinality discipline: run_id on traces and exemplars, flow and policy labels on metrics, so millions of runs don't explode the customer's observability bill.
  • coachCanary policy rollout: apply a new policy to 1% of matching runs in shadow, compare, then widen.
ACT III · NATURE'S SOLUTIONS

The hive already solved this

A short round, about three prompts, led by you. You picked the animal.

  • your callRun a short Nature's Solutions round (about three prompts, user-led) before converging.

The beehive: distinct roles, each bee knows where it goes and what it does; invaders are spotted fast and isolated quickly.⬢ your idea

  • coach insightTwo hive mechanisms worth stealing. Guard bees at the entrance check colony scent, identity by smell with no central registry. And intruders too big to remove are sealed in propolis where they stand: contain what you can't kill.

Colony scent: the run key

  • your ideaHekma's colony scent: registering, or being owned by hekma run, grants a unique run key that must ride on every request back to Hekma. It expires, and only the original process holds the refresh key.
  • your questionShould run keys be optional? Requiring them may break processes that fail to authenticate correctly with Hekma.
  • coachGuard like a hive: lenient during a nectar flow, strict in robbing season. The scent check is a policy: off, shadow (log unscented calls) or enforce (reject). Dev lenient; prod and regulated strict.
  • coachScent = the run key = the provider virtual key. One credential carries run identity and binding (the customer), so key custody, bypass prevention and contamination checks share one mechanism.
  • your callYou liked the three-level scent check: off / shadow / enforce.

Enforce (prod, regulated): the unscented call is turned away at the entrance. Keyed calls pass as always.

Propolis: make it loud, then seal only what you must

  • your ideaHekma's propolis is to make it loud: an anomaly warning in hekma ps, warn-level telemetry, and a response header telling the agent it is misbehaving or contaminated.
  • your insightThe responsibility split: the agent or its framework (CrewAI, LangGraph) corrects itself; ops act on the telemetry and build alerting around it. Hekma signals rather than kills.
  • coachGraduated propolis: loud (signal only), then seal the outbox (hold only irreversible customer-facing actions while the run keeps working), then seal the subject (block only calls carrying foreign identifiers), then a full hold. Policy picks the level per flow.
  • coachA self-heal contract: a contamination header names a standard remedy that frameworks can implement: drop memory, re-fetch context, resume from the last clean checkpoint.
1 · Loud
  1. Loud. Signal only: ps warning, warn telemetry, a response header. The run keeps going.
  2. Seal the outbox. Hold only irreversible customer-facing actions; the run keeps working.
  3. Seal the subject. Block only the calls that carry another subject's identifiers.
  4. Full hold. The whole run is sealed in place.
